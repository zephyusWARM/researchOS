# Program — Central Taiwan Food Radar

## Mission

Build a durable, evidence-backed food research line for **Taichung City + Changhua County** using 500碗 and MICHELIN Bib Gourmand as discovery signals, then independently verify whether each candidate is still open, still serving the relevant food, realistically visitable, good value, and worth spending a meal slot on.

The awards are **inputs, not verdicts**.

## Scope

### Current seed universe
- 2026 500碗: Taichung + Changhua.
- 2026 MICHELIN Bib Gourmand: Taichung.
- Historical backfill: 2023–2025 500碗 and prior MICHELIN retention for current candidates and notable former high-award shops.

### Geography rule
MICHELIN Guide Taiwan 2026 covers seven cities and **does not cover Changhua**. A Changhua restaurant having no Bib Gourmand status is therefore not negative evidence.

## Research questions

- Which current 2026 award candidates are still operating and serving the recommended dish?
- Which shops show persistent recognition across years or across independent guides?
- Which are one-off nominations versus durable local institutions?
- What does a realistic solo visit cost today?
- What are the real operational constraints: queue, sell-out, short hours, cash-only, ordering friction, branch ambiguity?
- What recent negative evidence could overturn an award-driven expectation?
- Which candidates are worth a dedicated trip versus only worth eating when nearby?
- How should candidates be clustered by geography and daypart for an efficient Taichung/Changhua food day?

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Award owner / official guide.
2. Restaurant-controlled menu, social account, reservation page, or official announcement.
3. Current structured business data for hours/location corroboration.
4. Recent independent reports with visit date and photographed/menu-backed evidence.
5. Community reviews as adversarial signals, never as sole proof of quality.

## Evaluation dimensions

Do not collapse everything into one prestige score. Track:
- award signal,
- repeat recognition,
- cross-guide agreement,
- current dish availability,
- realistic solo cost,
- value,
- logistics,
- queue/sell-out risk,
- freshness of evidence,
- recent negative evidence,
- geographic fit.

Use research priority to decide what to verify first. Research priority is **not** the same thing as restaurant quality.

## Persistence policy

GitHub is durable truth, not a live notebook. Batch meaningful Source → Evidence → Claim updates. Preserve source conflicts, aliases, stale prices, branch ambiguity, and uncertainty rather than silently resolving them.

Revalidate volatile hours, prices, and dish availability before giving a concrete visit recommendation.
