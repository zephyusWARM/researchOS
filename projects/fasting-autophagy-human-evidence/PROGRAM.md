# PROGRAM

## Core question

Does extending an overnight fast from roughly 14 hours to 16, 18, 24, or longer produce a distinct increase in human autophagic flux or an independent long-term health benefit?

## Evidence rules

- Prioritize human RCTs, controlled feeding trials, tissue biopsies, direct or defensible autophagic-flux measurements, and systematic reviews.
- Keep cell, rodent, healthy-adult, and disease-population evidence separate.
- Do not equate ketosis, lower insulin, mTOR signaling changes, or LC3/p62 biomarker changes with demonstrated autophagic flux.
- Explicitly control or account for calorie intake, weight change, diet quality, exercise, and circadian timing.
- Search for dose-response evidence across 12, 14, 16, 18, 24, and 36 hours rather than assuming a threshold.
- Distinguish “not shown to add benefit” from “shown to be ineffective.”
