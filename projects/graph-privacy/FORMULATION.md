# Working Formulation — Record-Level DP Is Not Topology Privacy

## One-sentence question

If many observed trajectories are generated by the **same latent interaction graph**, does trajectory-record-level differential privacy protect that shared graph, or does it protect the wrong unit of information?

## Setup

Let a latent interaction graph (G) generate a collection of trajectories

[
D_G = \{X_1,\ldots,X_m\}.
]

An NRI-style model is trained on (D_G) and learns a posterior over latent edges while also predicting dynamics.

The potentially sensitive object is not necessarily whether one trajectory (X_i) was in the training set. It may be **(G) itself**, or a particular interaction edge (e\in G).

## Privacy-unit mismatch

Standard record-level DP would define neighboring datasets by changing/removing one trajectory record (X_i).

But a topology-adjacent scientific question is different:

[
G \sim G' \quad\text{when the two latent graphs differ by one interaction edge.}
]

Changing one latent edge can alter the probability distribution of **many or all** trajectories generated by the system. Therefore trajectory-record adjacency and topology adjacency are different privacy notions.

This should not be described as “DP failing.” A record-level DP mechanism can satisfy its stated guarantee while still allowing accurate inference of a shared population-level / system-level property that its adjacency relation was never designed to hide.

## Research hypothesis

At matched trajectory-prediction utility, trajectory-record-level DP may reduce memorization of individual trajectories while leaving substantial latent-edge recoverability, because the topology is a repeated/shared signal across records.

The experiment should test this hypothesis rather than assume it.

## Minimal experiment

1. Generate many trajectories from a fixed 5-particle spring graph with known ground-truth edges.
2. Train a standard NRI baseline.
3. Train an NRI variant with trajectory-record-level DP-SGD, where each trajectory is one protected record.
4. Sweep privacy budgets and compare:
   - future-trajectory prediction MSE;
   - latent-edge accuracy / AUROC;
   - posterior entropy/calibration over edges.
5. Repeat across number of trajectories (m), graph density, trajectory length, and noise level.

### Key diagnostic

If edge recovery remains high while record-level epsilon is reduced, the interpretation is **privacy-granularity mismatch**, not violation of DP.

## Formal extension

The scientifically interesting extension is a topology-adjacent mechanism in which neighboring systems differ by one latent edge. Prior control work shows that topology can itself be the DP-protected object, but translating its sensitivity analysis to nonlinear NRI dynamics is nontrivial.

A second conceptual option is distribution/property privacy: prior work explicitly distinguishes confidentiality of global dataset properties from individual-record privacy.

## What would falsify the project hypothesis?

- Record-level DP destroys latent-edge recovery at the same rate it destroys useful trajectory prediction, leaving no distinct topology-leakage regime.
- A directly comparable prior paper has already established the same NRI-specific result.
- The per-trajectory DP training unit cannot be implemented without violating the accounting assumptions of the chosen optimizer.
- The topology-adjacent formulation collapses to a trivial or unusable privacy bound on the spring system.

## Scope boundary

The course project does **not** need to solve topology-DP for arbitrary nonlinear dynamical systems. A defensible contribution can be:
1. a precise demonstration of the record-vs-topology privacy mismatch in NRI, plus
2. a clearly separated exploratory topology-aware baseline.

Do not call the exploratory baseline “differentially private” unless its adjacency, sensitivity, mechanism, and privacy accounting are proved.
