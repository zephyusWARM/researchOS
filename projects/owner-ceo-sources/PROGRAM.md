# Program

## Mission

Identify the globally accessible, continuously producing sources that most effectively train durable **owner/CEO business thinking**: how an owner allocates attention, capital, talent, product bets, organizational design, incentives, and strategic risk under uncertainty.

The target is not “best business content,” “most famous guests,” or “most inspiring founders.” The target is repeated exposure to high-quality decision processes that transfer across companies and cycles.

## Research cutoff

**2026-09-19**

## Falsifiable sub-questions

1. **Operator proximity** — Does the source repeatedly expose real decision-makers and concrete decisions, rather than generic commentary?
2. **Causal depth** — Does it reveal constraints, trade-offs, mechanisms, and failed alternatives, not just retrospective narratives?
3. **Longitudinal auditability** — Can claims and frameworks be checked against a multi-year archive and later outcomes?
4. **Owner breadth** — Does it train product, market, capital allocation, hiring, organization, distribution, incentives, and strategy rather than one narrow specialty?
5. **Anti-halo robustness** — Would the source remain strong if famous guests, prestige branding, and viral episodes were removed?
6. **Failure exposure** — Does the archive include mistakes, reversals, postmortems, or disconfirming cases sufficient to resist survivorship bias?
7. **Continuity and usability** — Is there a durable publishing cadence/archive that a serious learner can repeatedly use?

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer first-party and primary material:
- official source/program pages and archives;
- original episodes, transcripts, essays, letters, case materials, shareholder communications;
- direct statements by operators/investors about decisions and outcomes.

Use independent commentary only for triangulation, criticism, or counterexamples. Search snippets, model memory, popularity metrics, and celebrity access are not evidence of quality.

## Candidate policy

Start broad across formats: operator podcasts, founder/investor essays, annual letters, case libraries, business-school interview series, company-building publications, and long-form operator media.

No candidate is protected by fame. Alternatives should be expanded until additional high-quality candidates stop changing the Core 3–5.

## Decision policy

Use hard gates before ranking:
- ongoing or durably recurring production;
- enough archive depth to sample across years;
- concrete decision content;
- materially more than guest prestige;
- globally accessible enough to be used repeatedly.

Then compare surviving candidates on transferability, causal depth, longitudinal auditability, cross-functional owner breadth, failure exposure, and signal-to-noise.

## Stop condition

Do not stop after finding plausible favorites. Stop only when:
- multiple search waves produce mostly redundant candidates;
- strong alternatives have been explicitly tested and eliminated or placed;
- red-team attacks on survivorship bias, celebrity halo, and guest-access ≠ quality are addressed;
- the Core 3–5 remains stable after at least one adversarial re-ranking;
- unresolved uncertainty is recorded in Snapshot/Coverage.
